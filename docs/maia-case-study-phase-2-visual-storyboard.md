# Maia Case Study — Phase 2 Visual Storyboard

Branch scope: fluxknight-homepage-rebuild
Status: Visual storyboard only. No Vercel deployment in this phase.
Design standard: skills/gpt-tasteskill/SKILL.md

## Visual direction

The case study should feel like a premium product story, not a stack of SaaS feature cards.

Core rules:
- dark cinematic Fluxknight visual system,
- large intentional spacing,
- image-led storytelling,
- one continuous prospect journey,
- minimal decorative filler,
- product-state visuals instead of generic icons,
- restrained motion,
- strong typography,
- mobile layouts designed deliberately rather than compressed from desktop,
- no fake metrics,
- no random glass cards,
- no repetitive six-column feature grids.

## Storytelling device

One prospect, Alex Morgan, moves through the entire page.

The viewer should always understand:
1. what Alex just did,
2. what Maia did in response,
3. what changed in the system,
4. what the human team now knows or can do.

Each chapter therefore uses three visual layers:
- customer-facing interaction,
- Maia action,
- operating-system state.

# Chapter 1 — Capture

## 03 — Campaign to Conversation

### Story
Alex sees a property launch campaign and chooses a CTA such as:
- Get launch updates
- Ask about availability
- Request the brochure
- Chat with our property assistant

### Visual
Split composition:
- left: ad / social / landing-page entry point,
- right: live WhatsApp-style or web-chat conversation.

### Conversation example
Alex:
I'm interested in the new development. When does it launch?

Maia:
I can help with that. I can also add you to the priority update list so you receive the launch details when they are available.

### Product state
A subtle CRM side panel appears:
- Lead created
- Source: Launch campaign
- Channel: WhatsApp
- Interest: Upcoming development

### Proof
The visual must clearly show that an identifiable conversation becomes a CRM lead.

# Desktop behavior

- Maximum narrative width should remain generous.
- Alternate between full-width cinematic visuals and split story panels.
- Do not use six-column card grids.
- Major visuals should occupy roughly 55–70% of viewport width.
- Keep each chapter visually distinct through rhythm, not random color changes.

# Mobile behavior

- Timeline becomes vertical.
- Chat and CRM stack, with the customer action shown first and system state second.
- Property recommendations become horizontally scrollable only if the cards remain readable.
- Handoff card becomes one stacked summary.
- System reveal becomes a vertical layered flow.
- No important story outcome may disappear on mobile.
- No text smaller than practical mobile readability.
- No horizontal overflow.

# Motion plan

Use motion only to clarify progression:
- fade/slide-in for new system states,
- CRM fields populate progressively,
- timeline indicators advance,
- handoff panel expands when reached,
- system reveal assembles in layers.

Respect reduced-motion preferences.

# TasteSkill acceptance criteria

Before implementation is accepted:
- no generic AI SaaS layout,
- no decorative card spam,
- no unsupported statistics,
- no excessive gradients,
- no meaningless glass effects,
- every card or panel proves a business capability,
- section spacing feels intentional,
- hero remains wide and readable,
- mobile keeps the complete story,
- visuals connect into one coherent product system,
- user-approved story architecture remains intact.
